# Coming Soon: type music video (round 01)

A kinetic-typography music video, 29.87 s, 1080×1920, music and effects only. Everything on screen is built from letters: she is a Q for a head, an A for her coat, an l for each arm and an L for each leg; the heap is the mono letters she deletes; the street is the web search's sources laid as paving, with f-shaped lamps and buildings that are big capitals; the seats are rows of n; the sign's frame is a ring of o bulbs; the doors, the paper and the screen are stretched capital I's; the moon is a C in a window that is a #.

The story, one transformation from A to B: she types "is it crazy to quit my job lol", deletes the "lol" she hides behind, which falls and lands large on the floor, the first word on the heap, then deletes the question word by word, and the words fall and pile up on it at her feet. Night after night the heap grows; she types "how do I open a cinema?" and drops that too, its letters landing warm on top. Then she types it again and sends it. The answers build the cinema: a web search lays the street, code draws a chart whose bars tip back into the rows of seats, four models answer "what do I call it?" and she picks The Lantern, an image model draws the sign. At night the conversation papers the glass doors from inside, writing facing her; from the street it is blank paper, a small red spiral, COMING SOON, and a taped note: "Not even we can read it." At dawn she peels the paper down and spells OPEN on the letter board with the o, p, e and n she once deleted. The camera dives through the O into the brand card.

## Grid and music

- 128.57 BPM: 28 frames a beat, a sixteenth every 7 frames, 4/4, 64 beats = 16 bars = 1792 frames (29.87 s at 60 fps). Every shot cuts on a whole beat; every hit, landing, slam and click lands on a beat, an eighth or (for the source chips, the chart's bars and the sign's bulbs) a sixteenth. The length is 64 whole beats rather than 30.00 s so the piece is exactly sixteen bars with an integer sixteenth.
- Review 1 kept the shot list and re-timed its middle: the names take four beats so each answer is held a beat (four answers, one per beat), the papering takes two tall sheets each held to be read, and the outside shot gave up a beat for them; OPEN's letters land half a beat later so every throw is seen, and the dive into the O is a sixteenth of silence.
- Review 2 restaged the opening and the end card: the film opens close on the composer and "lol" lands on the floor large on beat 1, with the camera whipping down after it; the end card is a sequence of reads, the mark, the wordmark alone for 42 frames, then one tagline line a beat on 58, 59 and 60, and its last 1.9 s sit still.
- D minor, resolving to F major at the close. The music leads; the picture answers it:
  - Bars 0–1 (doubt): a D minor pad and a long D in the 808 from frame 0, a soft kick on beats 1 and 3, the typewriter as the hats; deleting "lol" is a tick on the half beat and its landing on beat 1 an impact, a pluck, a tom and a sub thud; each word she deletes is a tick, and each lands as a pluck of the D minor pentatonic, falling A G F D C A G as the words fall.
  - Bars 2–3 (the heap's nights, the wish): Bb then C; the kit enters four to the floor with sixteenth hats; each whole-line drop is a snap, and its landing a pluck chord and a tom. A snare roll and riser climb from beat 14 into half a beat of hard silence (15.5) under her thumb's press, a reverse swell into the drop.
  - Beats 16–42 (the build, the drop on Send): Dm Bb F C Dm Bb, then half a bar of F; four-to-the-floor kick, claps on 2 and 4, open hats on the off-beats, a pumping 808 and off-beat supersaw stabs, a bell hook over every two bars. Every build event has its own sound: a tick per source chip (climbing), a tom per row of slabs, a pop per lamp and per chart bar (climbing), a whoosh on the tip, an impact as the seats land, a bell as the screen lights, a pluck per answer, a snap and bell on the tap, a downlifter as the others fall, a tick per bulb sixteenth, a tom and clap on THE, an impact on LANTERN, a bell chord as the bulbs light, a pop on her cheer.
  - Beats 42–50 (night): the kit drops out. F, then C under COMING SOON with a C major bell chord, then Dm; a snap and clap on each sheet that slaps onto the glass; a bell and pop on the note; a soft kick on the cut in; a tick as the stranger looks.
  - Beats 50–56 (dawn): Gm, Bb, C; the kick returns; a downlifter per peeled sheet; OPEN climbs D F A C on bells, one per letter, a clap on her cheer; a snare roll and riser into a sixteenth of silence (55.75) as the camera dives into the O.
  - Beats 56–64 (close): F major on the impact, the pad and a stab; bells F and A on the mark and the wordmark, then C, A and F falling to the tonic, one on each tagline line; the chord rings under the still card.

## Look

She is a Q for a head with a face in its bowl, all glyphs: dot eyes (o's when wide, carets when happy, chevrons when wincing, dashes when they blink), dash brows that tilt worried or set, and a mouth that is a dash, a tilde, an o or a parenthesis turned to smile or frown; seen from behind, the bowl is her hair. Her cheer is never a mirrored V: one arm goes up beside her head on the beat and the other out and up five frames later; an arm raised above her neck is drawn behind her head, so it never crosses her face. Motion blur takes four sub-frames, so fast letters smear rather than split into copies; every hit's squash and the camera kick read the whole frame, and a falling letter is gone from every sub-frame of its landing frame, so a hit never double-exposes, and the outside shot takes no kick, so THE stays in frame and the note inside the safe area. The post chain adds bloom (full on the night's lamps and bulbs, low where paper or type fills the frame), a small channel split on the blows and a deeper vignette in the two silences; it adds no flash, since the chain adds flash in linear light and even a little greys the whole frame. Light travels: night navy, a pale day in the time-lapse, a teal sky for the answer, ruled paper for the code and the names, the warm hall, the bulb-lit facade, cream paper doors, dawn, and the brand's warm charcoal.
