# All But One: beat-cut video (round 01)

A music video cut hard on the beat, in the vocabulary of the @IterIntellectus Western-civilization film: a HUD that never cuts, engraved gold line drawings on black and ink on parchment, type slammed in row by row with one keyword in a second colour, radial speed-line seams, counters that climb, and a build that tightens into every drop. Every copy row leaves with motion: over its last six frames it stretches wide, collapses to a line and smears forward, at full colour. The Devil's eyes are never twins: his left is larger, each lid blinks on its own clock, his grin is hitched higher on one side, and his horns differ; his expression changes only through an action with a key pose (the take at 39.5, the flinch at 48, the look up at 65, the scream's anticipation at 66). The HUD is the Devil's interview file and keeps his gold in act three; Signal Red marks only HushBox. The palette flips on the cut between ink and gold, parchment, radar cyan, hellfire and night; once HushBox enters it alternates the brand's warm charcoal with daylight paper, both carrying Signal Red, and the close is charcoal. Signal Red appears nowhere before the one token turns red on beat 42.

Nothing is kept from an earlier round: this is round 1.

## Grid and music

- 150 BPM, 24 frames a beat, 4 beats a bar, 75 beats = 1800 frames (30 s at 60 fps). Every shot cuts on a whole beat; the build's inner cuts land on half beats.
- The score is in E Phrygian (the flat second, F, is the Devil's) and resolves to E major on the mark. Act one is a trap groove under an organ riff (kick, clap on 2 and 4, 16th hats, a gliding 808). Beats 20 to 23.5 build: kicks on every beat, a snare roll rising in pitch, a riser, a stutter on beat 23. Beats 23.5 to 24 are silence. The drop (beats 24 to 39.5) is the groove doubled with supersaw stabs, a braam, glass, and a shriek for each demon that flies past the lens; a tape stop drops it into the silence on beat 40. Two heartbeats in the silence, then a bell on the one red token: the bell is HushBox's sound from then on, one on each value, and the E major chord lands on the mark.
