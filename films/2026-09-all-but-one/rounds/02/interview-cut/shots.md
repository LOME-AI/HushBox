# All But One: interview cut (round 02)

The film is an interview, 40 s long. The interviewer's questions type themselves onto the file ("DO YOU LIKE AI COMPANIES?", "ALL OF THEM?", "WHICH ONE?") and backspace themselves off before each cut, and the Devil answers in the first person. It opens straight on the first question and his answer. He praises the companies that keep, watch and leak: the watching eye shuts into a seam of light that cracks open, light forcing through it and shards breaking off its lips, and the secrets burst out of it as a flock of demons, each built differently, three of them carrying a secret each, that gathers into his laughing face with a halo of his collection round his head, one slot empty. The laugh freezes and his collection spreads into a field of hundreds of gold marks, every AI company. Asked "All of them?", his eyes throw searchlights and the camera flies through the field after them: mark after mark flares and takes his brand, faster and faster, until the whole field burns gold but one dark slot; in a beat of silence the camera rushes it, and on the drop it lights Signal Red, the HushBox mark, and a shockwave blows his field apart. He reaches for it and it burns him: "All but one." He lists HushBox's values in fear. Asked "Which one?", he screams and comes apart into one red dot, and the dot bursts into the mark. The end card then holds still for 5.6 s.

It is cut hard on the beat, like round 1's beat-cut take, and it moves from cartoon to menace:
- the grin is too wide for the face and holds two rows of teeth;
- the eyes carry iris rings and slit pupils that stay on the viewer while the head moves;
- the interview shots hold dead still until a hit snaps them into motion;
- the blacks are deeper;
- the score is sub drones, sour semitone stabs, screams swelling into the hits, and hard silence before the drop, the second question, ALL BUT ONE and the mark.

## Grid and music

- 112.5 BPM, 32 frames a beat, 4/4, 75 beats = 2400 frames (40 s at 60 fps). The film is the 30 s cut slowed to this tempo, not lengthened with material: every beat holds a third longer, while each hit's attack keeps its frame-counted snap. Every shot cuts on a whole beat; hits, grabs, stamps and stabs land on beats, half beats or quarter beats.
- E Phrygian:
  - The hook (0–4): a sub rumble, a semitone drone (E against F), heartbeats, and a typewriter strike per character of the question.
  - The praise (4–21): the organ riff over a quiet groove, a hit as the eye shuts and a crack as it splits.
  - The build (21–24): the crack opening on each hit over a rising roll, then half a beat of silence as it draws in, then a scream swelling into the drop.
  - The drop (24–33.75): a driven 808, sour detuned supersaw stabs, a braam, a shriek on each hero's lunge, a whoosh on the converge, the laugh. The kit stops on the freeze; the laugh's braam rings on.
  - The carry and the search (33.75–44): nothing restarts. A detuned drone swells in under the laugh's braam on the freeze and carries the question. The build starts on the dive (36): a kick that doubles each region (quarters, eighths, sixteenths), a strike and a pop on every flare, their pitch climbing, a whoosh and a bass hit on each whip, fire on 39 as the field ignites, a snare roll and a 4.5-beat riser into half a beat of silence (40.5), then the drop on the red mark (41): impact, braam, sub, glass and HushBox's bell. ALL BUT ONE on a low braam and drone; the claw's burn (impact, crack).
  - The values (44–60): an impact, braam and bell on each value, a tom and sub on each flinch, the heartbeat speeding up.
  - The end (60–75): the scream, a riser into silence, then the mark on E major with a bell on each read, the chord ringing under the still card.

## Kept from round 1, and why I would choose each fresh

- **The HUD frame** (`hud.ts`): kept as the founder asked ("i loved the frame around the best cut with the interview, secrets kept and bpm. keep that"). Its counter tells the story: SECRETS KEPT reads 0 on frame 0 and rises every frame from the start, faster as he talks through the praise; on the drop (frame 768), as the secrets escape, it jumps a hundredfold and keeps climbing through the flock; it stops when he freezes (frame 1080), and act three swaps it for his heart rate.
- **The hard cuts and the drops**: the founder asked for "the same energy", then for text slow enough to read. The cut is kept beat for beat and slowed from 150 to 112.5 BPM (a 32-frame beat), so every read holds a third longer while each hit still lands on its frame.
- **The engraved gold-line Devil** (`devil.ts`): kept for continuity with the take the founder ranked first, and made menacing: a grin too wide for the face with two rows of thin teeth, iris-ringed slit pupils that track the viewer, and unequal eyes blinking apart. His expression still changes only through actions with key poses: the grin snapping open, the burn, the eyes sliding away, the flinch, the scream.
- **Act three's structure**: a claim shot followed by a reaction cut, using the round-1 drawings (the red ring, the cipher archive, the lens, the SOLD stamp). The sweat shot is cut, so the end card can hold still. It is the structure round 1's review passed on its second pass: one read a shot, each value given its time. The encrypted shot scrambles the very secret the flock's first hero carried.
- **The type system** (`type.ts`): copy slams in with a smear and leaves by collapsing to a line at full colour. It is the round-1 grammar and passed review.
- **The leak's flock, from round 1's one-take-launch** (`flock.ts`): the founder asked for it by name ("I liked the single shot's scene from round 1 a lot more, use that scene here"). Its motion is brought across whole: the burst from a seam, every demon built from its own seeded design, each flapping, banking and reacting on the beat on its own, the hero demons carrying their secrets across the lens, the huge demon crossing it, the spiral converge into the Devil's face. It is redrawn in this take's gold line on the hellfire dark, retimed to 150 BPM, and it keeps Signal Red off: the bubbles start as paper and darken to shadow, the eyes are ember.
- **The radar eye and the archive**: kept as sets for the praise. The vault is gone: the eye now shuts and cracks, and the flock carries the secrets.

## Act three's light

Act three alternates charcoal and daylight paper on every cut: charcoal for the claims shown happening, paper for most of the Devil's reactions. Each cut changes the whole layout of the frame, and at most one light-dark change lands in any second (the engine's WCAG 2.3.1 rule measures at most 1 general and 0 red flashes in any second). The value headlines are set in the heavy sans, so each first line holds on one line at its size.

- The hook shows rather than tells: dark, the question typing, two eyes opening on it; then straight into his answer. The claw (`claw.ts`) is a long, bent arm with four jointed fingers of different lengths and a lagging thumb, each finger ending in a talon.

- The last question, "WHICH ONE?", is answered by the mark.
- Headlines arrive oversized and overshoot below full size once before they settle.
- Signal Red is HushBox's alone: the burn on the Devil's claw is white-hot, and nothing red is drawn on him.
- Every hit is keyed to its whole frame: event gates, the camera shake, the stamp, the padlock and the stab bubble are computed from the rounded frame, so both motion-blur samples draw the same pose. A hit lands complete on its frame and never double-exposes.
- The mark is drawn from `ctx.logo`: each of the logo's traced parts is filled from its own `Path2D`, carried by a transform that is the identity at rest, so the mark at rest is the logo file's own shape. The dot is the part nearest the file's centre. The parts fly out from it on their own springs (60) and are at rest from 61.5, and from there the end card holds still to the end: 5.4 s with no camera move, only the rays turning slowly and embers drifting. The round-1 stand-in drawing is gone.
